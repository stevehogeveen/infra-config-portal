Lab Builder · branch rack-workspace-visual-redesign

Strata is a beautiful shell with invented hardware. Lab Builder is plain chrome over proven truth.

Three ways to marry them — drawn with your real lab, so you can see exactly what survives contact with the data you actually have.

What the prototype at :3000 actually is

One self-contained component — app/StrataDesigner.tsx, 640 lines, plus globals.css. Every value is hardcoded: hostDrives: [6,6,6,6], Xeon Gold 6448Y, 1.0 TB DDR5, U.3 NVMe, dual-path 25GbE. No API calls; state persists to localStorage. It is plain React, so porting it into your Vite frontend is a copy, not a rewrite — but every number in it would have to be replaced, and your Gen10 lab with SAS behind a P408i-a doesn’t have most of those facts.

Option A

Reskin — Strata’s shell, your lab’s truth

Keep the chrome exactly. Swap every invented number for a probed one, and let the subsystems you can’t read say so out loud.

What you gain

  • The look you liked, immediately — it’s a port, not a redesign
  • Your rack survives as the left rail navigator
  • Strata’s outcome rail is a genuinely better home for vSAN readiness than a sidebar card

What it costs

  • Four of six subsystem tabs are empty on day one — a gorgeous shell mostly saying “not read”
  • Loses Strata’s actual purpose: profiles, what-if, Compare, undo/redo have nothing to act on
  • Tempting to fake the missing numbers, which is the one thing this product must never do
Option B · Recommended

Two modes — Design what you want, Build what you have

Strata becomes the planning mode; the rack workspace stays the proving mode. One toggle between them, and the gap between the two becomes the most useful screen in the app.

What you gain

  • Both products keep their purpose — nothing gets neutered
  • Drift is the payoff: “planned 18 vSAN drives, lab offers 8” is the single most useful sentence the app could say
  • Gives the kit form from your handoff a real home: member checkboxes and storage choice live in Design mode
  • Empty subsystem tabs stop being embarrassing — in Design mode they’re intentions, not missing readings

What it costs

  • Two surfaces to build and keep coherent
  • Needs a stored “design” document to diff against — new persistence, though the lab profile is most of it
  • Users must grasp the mode switch (mitigated: Build is the default landing)
Option C

Absorb — take the chrome, drop the configurator

Steal Strata’s frame and typography, throw away planning entirely, and give the whole right rail to the live cluster.

What you gain

  • Every panel is backed by a real endpoint — no empty shell
  • One screen, no modes to learn
  • Closest to what you already have, so the smallest build

What it costs

  • Throws away the configurator entirely — no profiles, no Compare, no what-if
  • The kit-design problem from your handoff stays homeless
  • Strata becomes a paint job rather than a capability

Recommendation

Option B, built in two steps so you get value before the second surface exists.

Step 1 — ship Option C’s screen. Port Strata’s frame, header, metric strip, and three-column layout into simplePages.tsx, with tabs named for endpoints you can answer. That alone gets you the look, keeps the rack, and breaks nothing — and it is the shared shell both modes will use.

Step 2 — add Design mode on top. The lab profile is already 80% of a design document: it stores cluster_member_device_ids, storage_location, vcenter_enabled. Point Strata’s configurator at that record instead of localStorage, and the drift panel falls out for free — because you now have a plan and a proof side by side.

That sequencing also fixes the thing your own profile is currently lying about: the kit named “vSAN Lab” is stored as single_server_local_storage with vCenter disabled and no members. In Option B that contradiction is visible on screen the moment you open Design mode.
