What was tried

A read-only check of the iLO's Redfish service (its standard management interface) at 192.0.2.27, with certificate verification on. The iLO answered, then this computer refused the connection because it does not trust the iLO's certificate.

What it means

The iLO's certificate is self-signed (made by the iLO itself), so this computer cannot confirm the iLO is the device it claims to be. It is not a password problem and not a network problem. The username and password were never sent.

What to check, in order

  1. Confirm 192.0.2.27 is the iLO you expect.
  2. Trust this certificate after comparing its fingerprint:
    Shows the fingerprint first; saves nothing until you confirm
  3. If you cannot compare it yet, you can turn off Verify TLS certificate for this iLO. The connection stays encrypted, but the iLO's identity is no longer checked. It asks you to confirm, and you can turn it on again once a trusted certificate is installed.
  4. Press Check connection again.

Designer's note

Audience

Professional operators who expect the precision of a bench instrument, and the lab technician who works beside them. They read a value, its unit and its source in one glance. Every technical word gets a short plain gloss the first time it appears on a screen.

Metaphor

A Rams / Braun / Teenage Engineering console. Off-white surfaces, graphite type, hairline rules on an 8 px grid, embossed keys, knurled steppers, and LCD readouts with ghost segments. The Step readout is a status display; the Blockers readout is a key that goes to the first blocker. The rack is a front view with U ticks and a dimension line.

Colour has one meaning each

Graphite: selected, switched on, and the screen you are viewing. Signal red: the one next key on a screen, the current step marker, and blockers that stop progress (with a square). Green: current proof only, bound to this server with its time (reached 08:35, iLO 5 v1.37, System ROM U32 v1.46). A recorded past result, such as the 24/09 erase, is graphite with its date and the words “recorded result, not a live check”. Saved-plan values are graphite too; toggles and decoration never use green. Amber: a disagreement or a change that deletes data. Limits that are simply not supported are muted text with a lock.

Type pairing

Archivo (400–700) for labels, headings and prose, a plain grotesk in the Akzidenz tradition. IBM Plex Mono (400–600) with tabular numerals for every readout, address, version, time and unit. Fallbacks: Helvetica Neue / Arial and Cascadia Mono / Consolas.

Narrow widths

Between 900 and 1280 px the fixed console scrolls inside its own frame, and each screen's next key and the Blockers readout move to the left so they stay in view. Below 900 px the console restacks: readouts in two columns, the step rail in two columns, every screen in one column, wide tables scroll inside their own box. Below 560 px everything is one column.

One grouped conditional PATCH to this server's SmartStorageConfig settings, with this body:
{ "DataGuard": "Disabled", "LogicalDrives": [], "Actions": [{ "Action": "PhysicalDriveErase", "ErasePattern": "SanitizeRestrictedOverwrite", "PhysicalDriveList": ["1I:1:1", "1I:1:2", "2I:1:5", "2I:1:6", "2I:1:7", "2I:1:8"] }] }
