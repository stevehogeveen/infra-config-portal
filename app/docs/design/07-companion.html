Designer's note

Audience

A complete non-IT person holding a tablet, who wants a calm colleague beside them. They should never have to guess the next step: the narrator says it, and offers exactly one filled button for it. Everything else on the screen is there to look at, with a one-line plain meaning at the first use of every technical word.

Metaphor

A guided conversation. The narrator's voice is in a serif; each state is one sentence and one button, at the top of the narrator column, with a dim "Coming next" line or a required item under it when that matters. Earlier sentences wait behind "How we got here". The main area shows the thing being talked about, in flat, square-edged panels that never blink.

Signature detail

On My Rack the narrator explains each label in words, behind "What the small labels mean". Five marks, the same everywhere: green with a time (this server said it today: reached 08:35, iLO 5 v1.37, System ROM U32 v1.46, nothing else), a tick with a date (a recorded result, not a live check), a pencil (in your plan, such as the 8 of 10 planned bays and the model), a hollow dot (suggested), a dotted outline (not known yet, such as today's drive count). Green never appears without a time from today, and never on switches or decoration.

Type pairing

Newsreader, a reading serif, is used only for the narrator's voice and this page's title, at 17 to 22 px, so the voice reads like a person. Atkinson Hyperlegible, drawn for low-vision readers, carries every heading, label, value and button in the screens. The system monospace appears only for the package name and the request body shown from the app's preview.

Tablet first

At every width the current sentence and its one button come first in the narrator, under a compact intro and a one-row journey strip, so the button sits inside the first 900 px at 1440, 1024 and 768 wide. Above 1100 px the narrator is a sticky column beside the screen; from 1100 px down it becomes a band on top and the screen takes the full width. The rack stacks above its card at 1200 px and below; two-column cards stack below 900 px. Only the rack drawing and wide tables scroll inside their own box. Every tappable control is at least 44 px.

What makes it distinct

Not an appliance wizard (Front Door), not pictures with labels (Picture Book), not a console. The difference is voice: the app talks, keeps its thread, and the interface is what it points at. Chalk paper, a margin column, a serif voice beside a plain sans interface, and one ink-blue button.
