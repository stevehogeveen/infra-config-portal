What changed from revision 1

Revision 1 was correct and quiet. This one keeps its structure and gives the product a face. A dark control deck carries the brand, the kit and the six-step progress strip, so the step rail is gone and the work area is wider. The rack is drawn as a technical elevation on blueprint grid, with rails, mounting holes, U rulers on both sides, LEDs, drive latches and switch ports. Headings use a grotesque with real character; data uses a mono face; primary actions sit on the orange the product already owns.

Rack customization stays, and gets better

The Add equipment screen keeps everything you can do today and makes each part more obvious: a hardware tray on the left with faceplate thumbnails, the rack in the middle with a live drop preview that names the slots and says what will move, a remove zone under the tray, a rack-height stepper in the header, and the edit panel on the right with name, model, slot, height, bay count and the bay grid. The DL360 keeps 10 bays and the DL380 24. Saving is still explicit, and the layout is still kept in the browser until you save it.

Eight rules the mockup follows

01The strip is the stepper

Six segments in the deck show done, current and blocked with their reason. It frees 240px of width on every setup page.

Left rail with six titled rows and captionsAdd equipment ✓ · Network ✓ · Connect · Firmware · Prepare ESXi (needs firmware) · Windows (needs ESXi)

02The heading is the status

No "Your next step" callout above a page that already has a title. The subtitle carries the live summary; the action sits in the rail or the header.

Your next step · Rack layout saved · Continue to connections…My Rack · 4 pieces · 8U · 1 server reached today

03Provenance is a chip

Every value shows where it came from in one small chip. Green stays reserved for proof with a time, as rule 3 of the build rules requires.

Saved address · Read-only Redfish probe completed · Not checkedsaved · proven 08:35 · blank

04Say the guardrail once

One dark guard card per screen says nothing touches hardware without preview and confirmation. Fields, buttons and footers stop repeating it.

Kit save only · No hardware touched · Read-only; no updates installed · Hardware changes require review and approvalPlan only. Nothing on hardware changes until you preview and confirm it.

05Size inputs to content

IPs, VLANs and MTUs get short fields in a two-column grid. Lists become compact rows with a remove mark and one add link.

Full-width input · "Up to 8 addresses." under every list260px field · limit shown only at the 8th row

06Collapse what is off

A switched-off service is one toggle row. Switching it on reveals its fields.

NTP server 1 · Add NTP server · Timezone (all disabled)NTP · off ⟷

07Results as banner and table

The verified erase keeps its three stages, evidence time and next action in one banner. Six tall rows become a six-row table with a result chip.

Six repeats of the same 20-word noteBay · Drive · Size · Method · Result · Plan

08Blocked stays visible with its reason

Locked steps show why in the strip, and a disabled next action names what unlocks it. Secondary actions live under "More", never removed.

Order of implementation

  1. Deck and strip. Replace the white top bar and the kit bar with the dark deck; render the six steps as the strip. The step rail component is removed from the setup shell and the work column widens.
  2. Rack drawing. Restyle the existing rack planner: blueprint stage, rails with holes, rulers both sides, LEDs and latches on faceplates, the drop-preview tip. Placement logic is untouched.
  3. Chips and guard card. One provenance chip component; one guard card slot in the rail; delete the "Your next step" callouts and per-field guard sentences.
  4. Forms. Two-column grids, sized inputs, toggle rows for DNS / NTP / SNMP.
  5. Storage, firmware, later steps. Banner plus table; device cards; rail holds order of work and prerequisites.
